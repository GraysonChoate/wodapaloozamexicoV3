Mexico City · December 4–6, 2026

Make a trip out of it

You’re coming to compete, but Mexico City has much more to offer beyond the competition floor. CDMX is the kind of city where your day can start with a workout, continue with tacos, coffee or a museum, and end at a rooftop, a great restaurant or exploring one of its neighborhoods. If you’re traveling from another city or country for Wodapalooza, save some time to experience it.

Where to stay

Roma

One of the best neighborhoods for getting the full Mexico City experience.

Roma Norte combines restaurants, coffee shops, bars, galleries, architecture and plenty of places you can discover on foot.

It’s a great choice if you want to experience the city’s food and social scene in addition to competing.

Best for food, coffee, bars, walking and nightlife.

Condesa

Right next to Roma, but with a slightly more relaxed atmosphere.

Parque México, Parque España and Avenida Ámsterdam make Condesa one of the most enjoyable areas to walk around, go for an easy morning run or simply recover after competing.

You’ll also have restaurants, coffee shops and bars within walking distance.

Best for walking, running, great food and having everything nearby.

Polanco

A more upscale option with a large selection of hotels, restaurants and shopping.

You’ll also be close to Chapultepec, Paseo de la Reforma and some of Mexico City’s most important museums.

Best for hotels, restaurants, museums, shopping and a more premium experience.

Santa Fe

Santa Fe can be one of the most convenient places to stay during Wodapalooza weekend, particularly if your priority is convenience and getting to and from event-related activities.

The area offers plenty of hotels, restaurants and shopping malls.

There is, however, a trade-off. Santa Fe is a much more modern and corporate part of Mexico City and doesn’t offer the same cultural variety, architecture, neighborhood atmosphere or walkability you’ll find in Roma, Condesa or Polanco.

Best for convenience during the event.

Our recommendation: if competing is your absolute priority, consider Santa Fe. If you want to turn Wodapalooza into a true Mexico City weekend, Roma, Condesa or Polanco will give you a much better opportunity to experience the city.

What to see

You don’t need an entire week to get a great taste of Mexico City. These are some of the landmarks worth adding to your trip.

Chapultepec

One of the city’s most important green spaces and a great place to combine sightseeing and culture.

Visit Chapultepec Castle and the National Museum of Anthropology, one of Mexico’s most important museums.

From there, you can continue toward Paseo de la Reforma.

Historic Center

If you only have a few hours for sightseeing, make this one of your priorities.

Start at the Zócalo and walk through the Historic Center toward Palacio de Bellas Artes and Alameda Central.

Within a relatively small area, you’ll find architecture, historic buildings, museums, food and some of Mexico’s most recognizable landmarks.

Paseo de la Reforma

One of Mexico City’s most iconic avenues.

Visit the Angel of Independence, continue toward Chapultepec or head toward the Monument to the Revolution.

It’s also a great connection between several of the neighborhoods you’ll probably visit during your stay.

Coyoacán

If you have half a day available, head south.

Coyoacán offers a completely different side of Mexico City, with plazas, markets, traditional streets, cantinas and coffee shops.

It’s also home to the Frida Kahlo Museum. If you want to visit, plan ahead and get your tickets in advance.

Polanco & Nuevo Polanco

Walk along Avenida Presidente Masaryk and Parque Lincoln, visit Museo Soumaya or simply use the area for restaurants, shopping and drinks.

Eat like a local

Food alone is a good enough reason to extend your trip. And you don’t need to book only high-end restaurants to eat incredibly well in Mexico City.

Try tacos al pastor, suadero tacos, quesadillas, chilaquiles, tortas, pan dulce and local markets. Roma and Condesa also have a huge specialty coffee, brunch and restaurant scene.

And after competing, tacos will probably taste even better.

Getting around

Uber and other ride-hailing apps are among the easiest ways for visitors to get around, especially if you’re traveling with a group, carrying competition gear or heading out at night.

But remember one important Mexico City rule:

Don’t calculate distance. Calculate time.

Something that looks relatively close on a map can take much longer depending on traffic. During competition weekend, always give yourself extra time to reach the venue.

Roma and Condesa are excellent neighborhoods to explore on foot. For longer distances, you can combine walking with Uber, Metro or Metrobús.

Arriving by air: check the latest airport rules regarding ride-hailing services and only use authorized transportation services or designated pickup areas.

Mexico City tips

Altitude
Mexico City sits more than 2,200 metres — around 7,300 feet — above sea level. If you’re coming from somewhere near sea level, consider arriving early enough to acclimate, stay hydrated and recover before competing.
Traffic
Don’t schedule activities too close together. A few kilometres can easily turn into a significant drive depending on the time of day.
Weather
Mornings and evenings can feel considerably cooler than midday. Bringing a light extra layer is a good idea.
Water
Stick to bottled or filtered water and, especially before competition day, be sensible about trying foods your body isn’t used to.
Payments
Credit and debit cards are widely accepted, but carrying some Mexican pesos is useful for markets, street food and smaller businesses.
Safety
As in any major city, keep an eye on your belongings in crowded areas and consider ride-hailing apps or authorized transportation when moving around at night.
Sunday on Reforma
If you have time after competing, check out the Sunday activities along Paseo de la Reforma. It’s a great opportunity to experience the city on foot, running or by bike.
